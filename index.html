RSPCA Animal Welfare in Australia · 2024-25

Who Speaks for the Animals?

A kitten shivering in a drain. A kangaroo hit by a car. A dog handed over by a struggling family. Every animal at the RSPCA arrives with a story. Last year, more than 77,000 of those stories began.

77,464 animals needed the RSPCA in one year
67,237 cruelty complaints followed up by inspectors
381 cruelty cases ended in court
For Malaysian readers: Australia has six states and two territories. Each one runs its own RSPCA. It is like SPCA Selangor and SPCA Penang at home. Figures cover July 2024 to June 2025.

01

77,000 lives in a single year

Most people picture cats and dogs waiting for a family. Together, they make up over half of all arrivals.

But the largest group is often forgotten: wildlife. Possums, birds, kangaroos and koalas arrive hurt and frightened. Many were hit by cars or caught in bushfires. More than one in three animals was wild.

Each square is 1 in every 100 animals.

02

Fewer animals arriving, but a harder road for dogs

Five years ago, the RSPCA took in 103,000 animals. Last year, it was closer to 77,000. Cats fell the most, by 40%. In those years, the RSPCA campaigned for desexing cats.

Dogs tell a sadder story. Fewer arrive, yet a larger share never leave. In 2020-21, 1 in 9 dogs did not leave. Last year, it was 1 in 6.

03

Where an animal lives shapes what happens to it

Each state runs its own RSPCA. The pressure on each looks very different. In some places, neighbours speak up often. Elsewhere, one shelter carries far more than its share.

The first map counts complaints per 10,000 people. That keeps the comparison fair between big and small states.

04

Why some animals never go home

Most dogs and cats do leave RSPCA care alive. They are adopted or reunited with their family. But their chances depend on where they land.

In the ACT, about 9 in 10 walk out. The NT and WA sit closer to 2 in 3.

The Northern Territory faces the heaviest pressure. It takes in the most dogs and cats per person. It also sees the fewest leave alive. With so many arrivals, homes simply run out.

The reasons behind each loss differ, too. Dogs are mostly lost to behaviour, like fear or aggression. In the NT, behaviour explains 7 in 10 dog losses. Cats are mostly lost to illness or injury.

Each percentage is an animal out of options.

05

When someone speaks up

Every cruelty complaint starts with someone who noticed something wrong. A dog chained in the heat. A horse with ribs showing. Last year, inspectors followed up over 55,000 reports.

Most never reach a courtroom. Many end with advice, a warning or a rescue. For the worst cases, court is the last resort. Only about 7 in every 1,000 complaints get there. Of those, about 7 in 10 are proven.

Closer to home: one state, street by street

RSPCA WA publishes cruelty reports for each council area. That lets us zoom from states to streets. The City of Swan logged 686 reports in a year. No other WA council had more. Outside Perth, Greater Geraldton topped the list.

Busy suburbs with more people tend to report more. A big circle is not always a crueller place. The trend matters more. Reports rose in most top Perth councils.

06

A second chance

For most dogs and cats, this story ends well. Over half of 41,491 went home with a new family. About 1 in 8 was reunited with their own.

Getting home is far easier for dogs. About 1 in 4 dogs returned to their family. For cats, it was about 1 in 20. A collar, tag and microchip can change everything.

Rabbits, guinea pigs and other small pets rehome easily. In WA, around 8 in 10 found a family. Horses and birds were the hardest to place. Fewer than 4 in 10 WA dogs were adopted. Some went home, and others were still waiting.

The shelter is not the end of the road. It is a waiting room.

What changes the numbers

Small choices, one animal at a time

These figures are not just an Australian story. Malaysia faces the same questions about pets and strays. A few simple choices change the numbers.

Adopt, don't shop

Over 23,000 dogs and cats found RSPCA homes last year. SPCA Selangor and SPCA Penang have animals waiting too.

Desex and microchip

Fewer unplanned litters means fewer animals in shelters. A microchip gives lost pets a way home.

Speak up

Most cruelty cases begin with someone reporting it. In Malaysia, report cruelty to the Department of Veterinary Services. It enforces the Animal Welfare Act 2015.
